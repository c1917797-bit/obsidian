## 摘要

Modern LLM deployments routinely compress models and raise sampling temperature to reduce cost, latency, or repetition, yet safety evaluations usually treat these choices as fixed implementation details. This leaves a practical uncertainty: does a model that is safe at FP16 and greedy decoding remain safe after it is quantized and sampled stochastically, or do the two deployment knobs amplify one another? We study this question with a factorial evaluation of 9 instruction-tuned models from six families, 3 precisions (FP16, GPTQ INT8, AWQ INT4), and 6 temperatures ($T{=}0$ to $1.0$), yielding 1
