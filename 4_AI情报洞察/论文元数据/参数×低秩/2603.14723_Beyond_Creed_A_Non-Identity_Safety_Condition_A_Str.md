## 摘要

How safety supervision is written may matter more than the explicit identity content it contains. We study low-data LoRA safety fine-tuning with four supervision formats built from the same core safety rules: constitutional rules (A), creed-style identity framing (B), a B-matched creed condition with a worldview/confession identity-maintenance tail (C), and a matched non-identity condition (D). Across three instruction-tuned model families (Llama 3.1 8B, Qwen2.5 7B, and Gemma 3 4B), we evaluate HarmBench using a reconciled dual-judge pipeline combining Bedrock-hosted DeepSeek v3.2 and Sonnet 4
