## 摘要

The IEEE 754 floating-point standard is the bedrock of modern computing, but its structural requirements -- a hidden leading bit, Base-2 bit-level normalization, and Sign-Magnitude encoding -- impose significant silicon area and power overhead in massively parallel Neural Processing Units (NPUs). Furthermore, the industry's recent shift to 8-bit formats (e.g., FP8 E4M3, OCP MX formats) has introduced a new hardware penalty: the strict necessity of Block-Scaling (AMAX) logic to prevent out-of-bound Large Language Model (LLM) activations from overflowing and degrading accuracy.
