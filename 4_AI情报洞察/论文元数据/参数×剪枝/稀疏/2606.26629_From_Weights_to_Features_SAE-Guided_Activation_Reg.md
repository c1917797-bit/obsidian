## 摘要

Weight-space regularization methods such as Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) are the standard approach to catastrophic forgetting in continual learning. However, those methods tend to underperform when applied to large language models. We argue that such underperformance can be partly explained by the ``polysemantic'' nature of large language models: per-weight importance estimates utilized by EWC-style regularization are too coarse and cannot isolate the knowledge that needs protection.
