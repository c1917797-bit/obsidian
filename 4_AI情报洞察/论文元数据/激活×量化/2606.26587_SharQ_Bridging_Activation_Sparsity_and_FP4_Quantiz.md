## 摘要

Low-bit floating-point formats and semi-structured sparsity are increasingly supported by modern accelerators, yet combining them for LLM activation compression remains challenging: activations contain input-dependent outliers that dominate block scales in FP4 quantization, and directly applying N:M sparsity masks discards moderate values, coupling sparsification loss with quantization error. We introduce SharQ, a training-free inference method that bridges activation sparsity and FP4 quantization through an online sparse--dense decomposition.
