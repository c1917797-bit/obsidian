## 摘要

Large Speech Language Models (LSLMs) typically operate at high token rates (tokens/s) to ensure acoustic fidelity, yet this results in sequence lengths that far exceed the underlying semantic content, incurring prohibitive inference costs. In this paper, we empirically revisit the necessity of such granular token-level processing. Through layer-wise oracle interventions, we unveil a structured redundancy hierarchy: while shallow layers encode essential acoustic details, deep layers exhibit extreme redundancy, allowing for aggressive compression.
