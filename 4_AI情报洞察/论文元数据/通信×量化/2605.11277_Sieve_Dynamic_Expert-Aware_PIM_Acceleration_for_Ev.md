## 摘要

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) has become a dominant architecture for scaling large language models (LLMs). However, the execution characteristics of MoE inference are changing rapidly and increasingly mismatch the assumptions underlying existing Processing-in-Memory (PIM) systems. Prior PIM systems for LLMs rely on static rules to offload memory-bound operations to PIM, without accounting for the combined effects of load imbalance and inter-GPU communication. Meanwhile, modern MoE models activate fewer experts out of increasingly many, creating a bimodal expert distribution: a small set of experts
