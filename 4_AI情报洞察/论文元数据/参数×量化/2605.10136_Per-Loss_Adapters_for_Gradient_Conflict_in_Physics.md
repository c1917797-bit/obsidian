## 摘要

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) train a single neural approximation by minimizing multiple physics- and data-derived losses, but the gradients of these losses often interfere and can stall optimization. Existing remedies typically treat this pathology either through scalar loss balancing or full-parameter-space gradient surgery, leaving it unclear which intervention is most appropriate. We show that PINN gradient conflict is not a uniform failure mode with one universal remedy. Instead, we identify distinct PINN gradient-conflict regimes, each associated with a different intervention
