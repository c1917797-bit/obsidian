## 摘要

Online imitation learning (IL), particularly on-policy distillation, has emerged as a strong LLM post-training approach, often outperforming offline supervised fine-tuning (SFT). Yet a principled understanding of when and why online interaction helps remains unclear. In this work, we challenge the view that error accumulation is the main source of online IL's advantage, and instead show that the benefits of online interaction depend critically on whether the setting is realizable, i.e., whether the student policy class can represent the expert policy.
