## 摘要

Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) has emerged as a parameter-efficient approach for adapting large pre-trained models, yet its behavior under continual learning remains poorly understood. We present a geometric theory characterizing catastrophic forgetting in LoRA through the lens of gradient subspace interactions. Our central finding is that forgetting is governed by a simple geometric law: $\mathcal{F} = α(1 - \cos^2θ_{\min}) + β$, where $θ_{\min}$ is the minimum principal angle between task gradient subspaces. This formulation reveals an approximate rank-invariance property, at high subspace angle
