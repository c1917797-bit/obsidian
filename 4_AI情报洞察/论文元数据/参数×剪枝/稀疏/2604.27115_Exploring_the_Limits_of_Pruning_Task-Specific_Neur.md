## 摘要

Neuron pruning is widely used to reduce the computational cost and parameter footprint of large language models, yet it remains unclear whether neurons in task-specific models contribute uniformly to task performance. In this work, we provide empirical evidence for the existence and importance of task-specific neurons through a systematic pruning study on language models specialized for mathematical reasoning and code generation. We introduce an activation-based selectivity metric to identify neurons with low contribution to the target task and prune them while preserving target-task accuracy,
