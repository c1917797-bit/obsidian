Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has catalyzed a leap in Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning, yet its optimization dynamics remain fragile. Standard algorithms like GRPO enforce stability via "hard clipping", which inadvertently stifles exploration by discarding gradients of tokens outside the trust region. While recent "soft clipping" methods attempt to recover these gradients, they suffer from a critical challenge: relying on log-probability gradient ($\nabla_θ\log π_θ$) yields divergent weights as probabilities vanish, destabilizing LLM training.
