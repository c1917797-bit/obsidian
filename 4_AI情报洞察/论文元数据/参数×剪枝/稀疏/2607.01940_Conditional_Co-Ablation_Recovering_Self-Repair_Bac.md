## 摘要

Mechanistic interpretability often relies on component-level interventions to discover how a model produces a behavior. This guides attribution, capability knockout, and model pruning downstream to operate by scoring each unit by the effect of ablation in isolation. Such first-order scoring is natural when component importance is additive, but becomes misleading when a transformer self-repairs: after a primary component is removed, a dormant backup can take over, muting the primary's measured effect while the backup itself appears irrelevant on the intact model.
