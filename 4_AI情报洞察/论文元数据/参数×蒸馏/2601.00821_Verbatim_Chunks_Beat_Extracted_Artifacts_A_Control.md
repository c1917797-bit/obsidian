## 摘要

A growing class of conversational-memory systems compresses dialogue history into structured artifacts -- extracted facts, decisions, or events -- on the premise that distilled structure retrieves better than raw text. We test this premise with a controlled ablation: within one fixed retrieval-rerank-reasoning pipeline, we swap only the stored representation -- LLM-extracted typed artifacts versus verbatim conversation chunks -- holding the model, retriever, reranker, and judge constant.
