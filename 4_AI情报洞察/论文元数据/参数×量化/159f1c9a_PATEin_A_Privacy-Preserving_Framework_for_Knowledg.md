## 摘要

In-context learning (ICL) enables task adaptation without modifying model parameters, making it well-suited for commercial large language models (C-LLMs) with closed-source constraints. However, ICL prompts often contain sensitive information, raising significant privacy concerns. Most existing privacy-preserving methods for ICL require access to model parameters, making them incompatible with C-LLMs. Recent methods based on teacher ensembles with differentially private aggregation have shown promise but face two fundamental challenges: ensemble inconsistency and limited knowledge integration.
