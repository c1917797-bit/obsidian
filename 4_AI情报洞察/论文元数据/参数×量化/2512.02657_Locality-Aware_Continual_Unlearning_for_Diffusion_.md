## 摘要

Real-world deployment of text-to-image diffusion models requires continual concept removal as new privacy, copyright, or safety obligations arise over time. Existing unlearning methods, however, are designed for single-step deletion and collapse after only 3-5 sequential applications. We trace this instability to two compounding factors: (i) coarse mapping targets that cause degradation to accumulate unnecessarily across steps, and (ii) the absence of local protection for semantically neighboring concepts, whose shared internal representations make them the first to suffer collateral damage.
