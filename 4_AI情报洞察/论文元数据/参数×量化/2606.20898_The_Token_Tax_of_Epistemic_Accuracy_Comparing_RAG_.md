Document-grounded assistants built on large language models are increasingly used in high-stakes, knowledge-intensive work. We investigate such a claim by comparing two grounding architectures: (a) retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) that retrieves a few relevant passages, and (b) long-context prompting, which loads the whole document collection in context.
